Who We Are

AbilityLink Impact Hub (ALIH) is being built with a long-term institutional approach: connecting underserved children, families, and communities with durable opportunities through evidence, dignity, and sustainable systems.

Institutional Philosophy

Beyond Temporary Interventions

AbilityLink Impact Hub (ALIH) is a community-oriented organisation being developed to connect people and communities with opportunities that strengthen education, protection, skills, livelihoods and participation in sustainable development.

ALIH is being built with a long-term institutional approach. Rather than assuming what communities need, we take time to listen, understand what already exists, identify practical gaps and use evidence to guide our decisions.

Our intention is not simply to start projects. We want to build an institution that can create lasting value for communities and continue serving people beyond individual projects or individuals.

Communities in Western Uganda—particularly in Kikuube District and Kyangwali Subcounty—face nuanced realities across host and refugee settlements. We reject standard NGO donor cycles that vanish when funding stops. Our institution exists to anchor generational opportunity.

Our Vision

«Communities where people can develop their abilities, access opportunity and participate in sustainable development.»

Our Mission

«To connect underserved children, families and communities with education, protection, skills, resources and opportunities through inclusive programs and partnerships.»

Institutional Longevity

Building for the Long Term: More Than Projects

ALIH is being built with a long-term institutional perspective. Success should not only be measured by the number of projects completed.

We want to build lasting community assets, institutional knowledge, and local leadership that thrives beyond the founders themselves.

We Want to Build:

People with greater opportunity
Communities with greater capacity
Young people with practical pathways
Children with stronger access to education and protection
Families with greater resilience
Local leaders with greater responsibility
Institutions with systems that work
Partnerships that continue

And ultimately: An institution capable of serving communities beyond the involvement of its founders.
